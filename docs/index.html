Points vs. lender credits, in plain English

Points are prepaid interest: you hand the lender cash at closing (1 point = 1% of the loan) and they hand you a lower rate. Points only make sense if you keep the loan long enough for the monthly savings to repay them — that’s exactly what the break-even month above tells you.

Lender credits are the mirror image: the lender covers your closing costs, but charges you a higher rate to do it. A “no-closing-cost refi” isn’t free — you pay for it every month for years. Use the toggle above and compare honestly.

Rule of thumb this tool enforces: never pay points you won’t outlive. If the break-even month lands after your horizon, the points were a donation.
